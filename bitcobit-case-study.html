01 / THE CHALLENGE

The first brief was too much product.

The original concept combined real crypto trading, a payment card, staking and a wallet. For a solo designer, it introduced dependencies on regulated services, custody and commercial partners before the core learning experience could be tested.

The beginner problem was simpler: how do you learn the mechanics of trading without using real savings to discover your mistakes?

iOS firstSolo designerNo real-money transactionsFocused first release
02 / THE PIVOT

Change the product definition before polishing the interface.

I reframed the concept as a simulator: users practise with virtual funds while following market prices. The shift made learning the purpose of the product, rather than a step toward a real-money transaction.

INITIAL CONCEPT

A real-money neobank

Trading, card payments, staking and custody.

REVISED DIRECTION

A practice environment

Virtual funds, order practice, portfolio feedback and simulated recurring plans.

This is a product-scope decision, not a legal determination. Any future launch would still need a review of applicable requirements.

03 / DISCOVERY

Look for a learning gap.

The source describes desk research and a review of Binance Demo, Bybit Demo, Pi42, Delta Exchange and TradingView paper trading. The design hypothesis was that a beginner-focused experience could make practice easier to understand than a trading interface built primarily for experienced users.

Clarity

Can users distinguish practice funds from real money?

Guidance

Can they understand an order and its outcome?

Trust

Does the interface explain market-data and account limits?

These were inputs to the design, not findings from completed user interviews.

05 / DESIGN DECISIONS

Every addition needed a reason.

  1. 01

    Make practice explicit

    A required onboarding explanation and persistent practice labels add a step, but address the most important trust question.

  2. 02

    Label the data source

    Live market data is the intended default. A clearly labeled simulated fallback is proposed for interruptions.

  3. 03

    Explain limits at the point of use

    The proposed Free tier caps individual trades at $2,000 and daily activity at $10,000. The limit message should explain what happened before offering Prime.

  4. 04

    Use a focused visual system

    An OLED-inspired dark palette supports the product direction. Contrast checks determine usable text colors.

  5. 05

    Cut the first release

    The source describes reducing 28 feature areas to about 10. Tax reports, NFTs, hardware wallets and a DApp browser were outside the proposed core.

11 / DESIGN SYSTEM & FOUNDATIONS

A dark foundation built for live trading.

The BitcoBit visual language pairs a high-contrast dark foundation with a vibrant lavender brand accent, deliberate green/red trading feedback, and multi-tier surface elevations designed specifically for mobile OLED screens.

Core color system

Brand Lavender #B772FF · Primary accent & active states
Deep Canvas #0A0B0D · Base OLED background
Buy / Bullish #00C076 · Trade execution & profits
Sell / Bearish #FF3B30 · Order sell & stop loss
Elevated Surface #141518 · Cards, sheets & modules
Control Surface #1C1D24 · Preset chips & inputs
Secondary Tint #D8B4FF · Progress & badges
High-Contrast Text #FFFFFF · Headlines & numbers

All colors match the live BitcoBit iOS prototype with AA contrast ratios on dark surfaces.

iOS System Palette
01 · Semantic Hierarchy

Purpose-Driven Roles

Action buttons, state toggles, and status badges draw from semantic tokens rather than hardcoded hex values.

02 · Surface Depth

Three Elevation Layers

Canvas (#0A0B0D) → Card (#141518) → Interactive Control (#1C1D24) create effortless visual depth without harsh borders.

03 · Trading Feedback

Instant Affirmation

Mint green (#00C076) and coral red (#FF3B30) reserve high-saturation moments exclusively for market movements and order status.

04 · Accessible Contrast

WCAG AA Compliant

Dark ink on primary lavender buttons achieves a 9.2:1 contrast ratio, ensuring readable interactions in all lighting.

12 / COMPONENT LIBRARY

Modular components built for live market feedback.

A financial practice simulator requires rapid information retrieval, unmistakable feedback loops, and touch ergonomics engineered for volatile market moves. I structured 30+ production component variants across 5 modular categories—optimized specifically for OLED dark surfaces, tap target precision, and live state clarity.

01

Hit Target Ergonomics

Every interactive trigger meets Apple HIG standards with a minimum 44×44 pt touch target, mitigating mis-taps during fast market swings.

02

Semantic State Color

Primary Lavender (#B772FF) handles general actions, while Buy Mint (#00C076) and Sell Crimson (#FF3B30) reserve high saturation strictly for financial intents.

03

Tactile Micro-States

Rest, active, focused and disabled states communicate clear tactile boundaries so beginners never second-guess whether an action executed.

01

Information Scent

Balances lead with dominant font scales ($13,250.00), paired with immediate contextual actions like Add Cash and First Trade entry pills.

02

Market Density

Asset rows package token logos, ticker names, live prices, 24h percentage deltas and inline sparklines within a clean 64px vertical cadence.

03

Strategy Consistency

Automated DCA bots and copy-trading cards share modular card primitives—highlighting schedule cadence, invested capital and return rate.

01

Gradient Spline Scrim

Smooth bezier price curves with low-opacity gradient fills establish visual depth while haptic scrubbing markers track timestamps with clarity.

02

Radial Diagnostic Gauge

The 83/100 circular score ring turns complex portfolio risk metrics into an instant, beginner-friendly diagnostic visualization.

03

Rebalancing Guidance

Thumb-Zone Dock

A swipeable bottom modal sheet centralizes Buy, Sell, Convert, Send, Receive and Withdraw actions in one recognizable menu pattern.

Personalized Risk Toggles

Segmented profile selectors (Conservative, Balanced, Growth) calibrate simulated automation rules without requiring dense finance jargon.

Virtual Practice Card

15 / REFLECTION

The strongest design decision was the pivot.

A clearer product definition made the interface easier to explain. Removing real-money features created room to focus on practice, feedback and trust.

Scope is design

Features that dilute the practice story can weaken the whole experience.

Trust needs repetition

Practice labels and honest data states should reinforce the same promise throughout the journey.

Audit the tokens

A visually convincing dark theme can still contain low-contrast text.

Next: run the study, address tertiary-text contrast, verify the product’s launch requirements and investigate an Android version.
